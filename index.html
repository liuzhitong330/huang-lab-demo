Display enrichment is useful for finding possible binders, but it does not answer the question that matters before committing expression and purification capacity: which candidates still look strong when binding, dissociation, reproducibility, and sample-quality evidence are considered together?

I built a small decision layer over the Huang Lab's ADAPT-M study. It exposes the filters instead of hiding them, links the shortlist back to the reported measurements, and adds two practical checks: SEC trace inspection and backbone-conformer sensitivity from the lab's Caliby repository.

How it works

Design–build–test handoff
Choose a small, defensible panel for orthogonal validation

The default panel asks for sub-500 nM median affinity, a dissociation AUC of at least 20 seconds, reported enrichment above 4.5, and at least eight affinity replicates. Those cutoffs are editable below; they are a triage policy, not biological truth.

The published study reports 14 true binders from 51 sequences measured with ADAPT-M. This demo harmonizes 13 canonical design IDs across the affinity, dissociation, enrichment, computational-metric, and sequence tables. The separate s19901_ch construct is not merged into its parent design.

Analysis 2 — SEC is a separate quality question

Protein-sample check
Inspect the trace before treating affinity as development readiness

The strongest binder is not automatically the cleanest sample. Select one of the five monobody–meGFP constructs used in the paper's validation experiments.

Trace:

At these descriptive settings, s19382 has the best median affinity and slowest dissociation, but its reported SEC trace also has the largest pre-main-peak area proxy among the five fusion proteins. s14335 is weaker in affinity but has a cleaner-looking trace by that same proxy. That tension argues for carrying both forward, rather than letting one score decide the panel.

The practical implication is simple: preserve a per-conformer score or disagreement flag in the experimental handoff, especially around the mobile region centered on residues 16–22 in this example.

Why this matters for the lab

A proposed next validation panel

Under the default gate, the first panel is s19382, s14335, s19901, and s37711. I would express and purify them in parallel, record yield and soluble recovery, inspect SEC using a predeclared integration rule, and then run an orthogonal affinity assay. The panel retains the two leads highlighted in the paper while adding two candidates with intermediate affinity and dissociation behavior, giving the next experiment more information than repeating only the top hit.

What I would bring

My relevant overlap is the handoff between computational design and interpretable experiments: I have used AlphaFold 3 and ProteinMPNN for structure-guided candidate work, and I have hands-on experience developing HPLC/SEC methods, running protein and cell assays, checking data quality, and maintaining reproducible records. Methods note

All experimental values come from the open-access ADAPT-M paper and Source Data workbook. The article reports that display enrichment can contain non-binders and that 14 true binders were measured; this demo recomputes medians and quartiles from the workbook and joins 13 canonical IDs across relevant tables. Computational metrics are shown only as supporting context, never as substitutes for measured binding. The conformer view uses files from the Huang Lab's Caliby repository at the recorded commit. Cutoffs and weighted scores are exploratory decision aids, not validated predictors. No new wet-lab data were generated.
